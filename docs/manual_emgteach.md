## 0. Executive summary (one page)

**emgteach** is a desktop application for the **acquisition, analysis and
normalisation of surface electromyography (EMG) signals** in the physiology
teaching laboratory. It lets you:

- **Record** muscle electrical activity in real time with low‑cost hardware
  (BITalino or Arduino + MyoWare), on **one or two channels** (e.g. an agonist
  and an antagonist muscle).
- **Visualise** the raw, filtered and envelope signals live, mark events
  (manually or automatically) and **monitor muscular load in real time** with
  tiredness/fatigue warnings.
- **Analyse** a recording offline: amplitude (RMS), spectral content (PSD, mean
  and median frequency) and **muscle‑fatigue indicators**.
- **Normalise** the signal to the **Maximum Voluntary Contraction (MVC)** and
  assess **muscular load** with Jonsson's method (ergonomics).
- **Export reproducible PDF reports** of the session.

Files are saved in the standard **EDF+** format (compatible with other tools such
as MNE‑Python or EDFbrowser).

The application is organised into **three tabs**: **Acquisition**, **Analysis**
and **MVC** (normalisation to the maximum contraction).

## 1. Physiological basis of surface electromyography (sEMG)

### 1.1 What is measured

**Surface electromyography** records, through electrodes placed on the skin, the
**electrical activity generated by muscle fibres** as they contract. It does not
measure force or movement directly, but the **electrical command** the nervous
system sends to the muscle.

### 1.2 Origin of the signal

- The functional unit is the **motor unit**: a motor neuron and all the muscle
  fibres it innervates.
- When the motor neuron fires, its fibres depolarise and generate a **motor‑unit
  action potential (MUAP)**.
- The surface electrode picks up the **sum** of the MUAPs of the nearby motor
  units. The resulting signal looks **stochastic** (noise‑like), with typical
  amplitudes from **tens of µV to a few mV**.

### 1.3 What information the signal carries (amplitude and frequency)

The EMG signal carries two complementary kinds of information:

- **Amplitude** (how "large" the signal is): reflects the **level of muscle
  activation** — how many motor units are recruited and how fast they fire. It
  is related (non‑linearly) to the **force** produced. It is quantified with the
  **envelope** or the **RMS** value.
- **Frequency** (how the energy is distributed across the spectrum): reflects,
  among other things, the fibres' **conduction velocity** and the recruitment
  pattern. It is the basis of **fatigue detection** (see §5.4). It is studied with
  the **power spectral density (PSD)** and the **mean (MNF) and median (MDF)
  frequencies**.

### 1.4 Artefacts and noise (why we filter)

The raw signal contains components that are not useful muscle activity:

- **Mains interference** at 50 Hz (in Europe) and harmonics.
- **Electrode movement** and baseline drift (low frequency).
- High‑frequency **noise**.

That is why the signal is **filtered** before it is interpreted (see §3).

## 2. Hardware and setup

### 2.1 Supported devices

| Device | Connection | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **BITalino (revolution)** | Bluetooth | Biopotential platform. Requires Python ≤ 3.11. |
| **Arduino RedBoard Plus + MyoWare 2.0** | USB serial | Open‑hardware EMG sensor; works on every supported version. |

Both are handled interchangeably from the application (the device type is chosen
in the Acquisition tab).

### 2.2 Electrode placement

- Electrodes over the **muscle belly**, aligned with the fibre direction, with a
  **reference** electrode on an electrically neutral area (e.g. a bony prominence).
- Clean skin to lower impedance and reduce artefacts.

[Suggested figure: photo/illustration of electrode placement on a muscle (e.g.
biceps brachii) labelling the active and reference electrodes.]

### 2.3 One or two channels (agonist / antagonist)

The application supports **1 or 2 simultaneous channels**. The two‑channel case is
useful to study **agonist/antagonist pairs** (e.g. biceps and triceps): it lets
you observe **co‑activation** and the coordination between muscles. Each channel
has an **editable label** (muscle name) and a fixed **colour** (channel 1 = blue,
channel 2 = red), consistent across all plots.

### 2.4 Units and sampling

- The signal is expressed in **millivolts (mV)**.
- The nominal **sampling rate** is **1000 Hz** (1000 samples per second per
  channel), enough to cover the informative EMG band.

## 3. Signal‑processing chain (DSP) and its meaning

From the **raw** signal, the program computes a processing chain in real time (and
recomputes it during analysis). Each step has a physiological meaning:

1. **50 Hz notch filter** — removes **mains interference** (leaving the real
   muscle activity "clean").
2. **20–450 Hz band‑pass filter** — keeps the **informative EMG band** and
   discards baseline drift (< 20 Hz, electrode movement) and high‑frequency noise
   (> 450 Hz).
3. **Rectification** — takes the **absolute value** of the filtered signal; since
   the activity is symmetrically positive and negative, rectifying lets us measure
   its "magnitude".
4. **Envelope (5 Hz low‑pass filter)** — smooths the rectified signal into a curve
   that follows the **activation level** over time (the "intensity" of the effort).
5. **RMS (Root Mean Square)** — another amplitude measure, computed in windows; it
   is the classic indicator of EMG **amplitude** and relates to activation/force.

**Important (file design):** only the **raw signal** is stored in the EDF (one per
sensor). The filtered signal and the envelope are **deterministic functions** of
the raw signal and are **recomputed** on analysis; this keeps the file smaller and
always reproducible.

### Default processing parameters

| Parameter | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Sampling rate | 1000 Hz | Samples per second |
| Band‑pass | 20–450 Hz | Informative EMG band |
| Notch | 50 Hz | Mains interference (Europe) |
| Envelope cutoff | 5 Hz | Smoothing of the activation level |
| RMS window | 50 ms | RMS amplitude computation |
| Fatigue analysis segment | 1 s (50 % overlap) | Windows for RMS/MDF over time |
| Percentile for the MVC reference | 95 | Robust maximum reference (see §5.1) |

## 4. The interface: the three tabs

### 4.1 **Acquisition** tab (real time)

Used to **record** a session and watch it live.

**Device configuration**
- Device type (BITalino or Arduino + MyoWare).
- **MAC** address (BITalino) or **COM port** (Arduino).
- **Destination folder** for the EDF file.
- **Number of channels** (1 or 2) and each channel's **label** (muscle name).

**Acquisition control**
- **Connect/Disconnect** and **Start/Stop recording** buttons.
- A communication **status LED**: red (disconnected), yellow (connected, no
  traffic), green (receiving data). A *watchdog* forces a disconnection if the
  device stops sending data (e.g. a lost Bluetooth link).

**Real‑time plots** (two):
- **Raw EMG signal** (mV).
- **Envelope**.

> The filtering (50 Hz notch + 20–450 Hz band‑pass) is still applied to compute
> the envelope, but the intermediate filtered trace is **no longer drawn**: for
> physiology teaching the raw signal and its envelope are enough.

With **two channels**, the raw plot is shown **stacked** (one "lane" per channel,
in its colour), while the envelope — being non‑negative — is **overlaid** so the
two muscles can be compared directly. Each plot's vertical
scale is adjusted with **▲▼** buttons, and the **time window** with a zoom
dropdown and ◀▶ buttons (also with the mouse wheel).

**Event markers**
- **Manual** markers with preset labels (*Contraction onset*, *Contraction end*,
  *Fatigue*, *Rest*, *Other…*) or custom ones; insertable with a button or the
  **M** key.
- **Automatic contraction‑onset detection** (*Auto‑onset*): the program marks the
  start of a contraction when the envelope crosses a **threshold** (resting
  baseline + *k*·standard deviation); the sensitivity is set with the **k**
  parameter. (Meaning in §5.6.)
- Markers are drawn live on the plots and stored as **annotations** in the EDF.

**Live muscle‑load monitor (real‑time MVC)** *(new)*
- **Calibrate MVC** button: during recording, the subject performs a few seconds
  of **maximum contraction**; the program computes the **MVC reference** per
  channel.
- After calibration, each channel shows a **load bar** indicating the **current
  % MVC**, with **colour zones**: green (normal), orange (*Warning* — tiredness)
  and red (*Danger* — fatigue), plus the live **P10/P50/P90** levels
  (static/median/peak). (Meaning in §5.5.)

### 4.2 **Analysis** tab (offline)

Analyses a saved EDF recording in depth.

**Parameters**
- Selection of the **EDF file** and the **channel** to analyse.
- **Envelope cutoff frequency** (editable).
- Optional **student** name and **code** (for the report).

**The 8 analysis panels** (selectable) and their meaning:

| Panel | What it shows | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **1A. Raw signal** | Unprocessed EMG | Starting point; lets you spot artefacts |
| **1B. Filtered + rectified** | Clean signal and its absolute value | Isolates the real muscle activity |
| **2. Envelope vs RMS** | Two amplitude measures overlaid | Activation level over time |
| **3. Normalised envelope** | Envelope scaled to its maximum (0–1) | Shape of the activation, comparable |
| **4. PSD with MNF/MDF** | Power spectral density | Energy by frequency; basis of fatigue |
| **5. RMS per window** | RMS amplitude over time | Evolution of the effort |
| **6. MDF vs time (fatigue)** | Median frequency per segment + trend | **Fatigue indicator**: if it falls, there is fatigue |
| **7. RMS vs MDF** | Amplitude–frequency relationship | Force/fatigue relationship during the task |

### 4.3 **MVC** tab (normalisation to the Maximum Voluntary Contraction)

Expresses the signal as a **percentage of MVC** and assesses **muscular load**.

**Inputs**
- **Test EDF** (the task to normalise) and, optionally, an **MVC reference EDF** (a

**The 3 time‑series panels**
1. **Filtered and rectified** signal.
2. **Envelope** with the **MVC reference amplitude line**.
3. **Normalised** signal (% MVC), with the 100 % line.

- A structured **data panel**: MVC reference, source, **mean activation** and the

  **red** when out of range), its **normal range** and a **short explanation** of
  its meaning.

**MVC normalisation PDF report**: like in Analysis, with time‑range selection; it
includes the panels, the APDF chart and the load‑metrics table.

## 5. Physiological concepts and metrics (interpretation guide)

### 5.1 Maximum Voluntary Contraction (MVC) and normalisation

The **MVC** is the maximum activation a subject can voluntarily generate in a
muscle. It serves as the **reference** to express any other activation as **% MVC**.
Normalisation matters because the absolute EMG amplitude (in mV) depends on
non‑physiological factors (skin impedance, exact electrode position, anatomy);
**% MVC** allows **comparison** across muscles, subjects and sessions.

> The MVC reference is computed as the **95th percentile** of the envelope (not the
> absolute maximum), so it is **robust** against artefacts or spurious peaks.

### 5.2 Amplitude measures: RMS, iEMG, envelope

- **Envelope**: smoothed curve that follows the **intensity** of the instantaneous
  activation.
- **RMS (root mean square)**: the standard amplitude measure in a window; it
  relates to the **recruitment level** and, non‑linearly, to **force**.
- **iEMG (integrated EMG)**: the **area** under the envelope; it quantifies the
  **total activity** accumulated over a period (total effort).

### 5.3 Spectral content: PSD, MNF and MDF

- **PSD (power spectral density)**: shows **how the signal's energy is distributed
  across frequencies**.
- **MNF (mean frequency)** and **MDF (median frequency)**: two summaries of the
  spectrum. The MDF is the frequency that **splits the spectrum into two halves of
  equal power**; the MNF is the spectrum's "centre of gravity". Both **fall with
  fatigue**.

### 5.4 Muscle fatigue and its detection

During a sustained contraction, the muscle **fatigues**: fibre **conduction
velocity** drops and the recruitment pattern changes. Electrically, this shows up
as a **shift of the spectrum toward lower frequencies** (*spectral compression*) —
i.e. **MNF and MDF decrease over time**.

- The program estimates fatigue by fitting **MDF against time** (panel 6 of
  Analysis) and looking at its **slope**:
  - **Negative** slope (MDF falls) → **fatigue detected**.
  - Flat or positive slope → **no fatigue**.
- Fatigue is often accompanied by an **increase in amplitude (RMS)** to keep the
  force, hence the interest of panel 7 (RMS vs MDF).

[Suggested figure: illustration of the spectral leftward shift with fatigue (two
PSDs: start vs end) and a descending MDF‑over‑time trace.]

### 5.5 Ergonomic muscular load: Jonsson's method (APDF)

To assess the **sustained muscular load** during a task (ergonomics, biomechanics,
sport) we use **Jonsson's method** — the analysis of the **Amplitude Probability
Distribution Function (APDF)** of the envelope normalised to % MVC. It is a
**published, public‑domain** method (Jonsson, 1978/1982). From the cumulative
distribution, **three load levels** are read:

| Level | Percentile | Definition | Meaning | Guideline limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Static** | P10 | Load exceeded **90 %** of the time | Near‑continuous background load | ≤ ~5 % MVC |
| **Median** | P50 | Load exceeded **50 %** of the time | Typical working load | ≤ ~14 % MVC |
| **Peak** | P90 | Load exceeded **10 %** of the time | Recurrent high efforts | ≤ ~70 % MVC |

- Sustainedly exceeding these limits is associated with **fatigue** and a higher
  risk of **musculoskeletal disorders**.
- In the **live monitor**, the current load is classified into **zones**: *Normal*
  (green), *Warning* / tiredness (orange, by default above ~40 % MVC) and *Danger*
  / fatigue (red, by default above ~70 % MVC). This enables **intervention** (a
  pause, a posture change) before fatigue sets in.

> **Note.** The default limits (static 5 %, median 14 %, peak 70 %; warning 40 %,
> danger 70 %) are **guideline values** derived from the literature, **not clinical
> thresholds**; they can be adjusted.

[Suggested figure: annotated APDF chart with the cumulative curve and the
static/median/peak points; alongside, the live‑monitor bar with the green/orange/red
zones.]

### 5.6 Contraction‑onset detection

A **contraction onset** is detected automatically when the envelope crosses a
**threshold** defined as the **resting‑baseline mean plus k·standard deviations**,
with a minimum dwell time (debounce) and a refractory period to avoid marking the
same event twice. The **k** parameter controls the **sensitivity** (smaller k =
more sensitive).

## 6. File formats and reproducibility

### 6.1 EDF+ and reliable writing

Recordings are saved in **EDF+** (*European Data Format*), a widely used
electrophysiology standard readable by tools such as **MNE‑Python** or
**EDFbrowser**. emgteach uses a **buffered‑write** pattern that avoids a silent
corruption failure that can occur when writing EDF during real‑time streaming
(described in a methodological publication by the author).

### 6.2 Annotations (markers)

Markers (manual and automatic) are stored as **EDF+ annotations**, so the
recording's context (contraction onsets, fatigue, rest…) travels **inside the file
itself** and is recovered on analysis.

### 6.3 PDF reports

Reports are **self‑contained and reproducible**: they include the chosen graphs, a
metrics table and a **footer with the program version and the generation date**,
and are saved automatically next to the source EDF.

## 7. Typical workflows (step by step)

### 7.1 Full session: acquire → analyse → normalise → report

1. **Acquisition.** Connect the device, choose 1–2 channels and labels, *Start
   recording*. Have the subject perform the task; mark relevant events (manually or
   automatically). *Stop recording* (the EDF is saved).
2. **Analysis.** Open the EDF in the Analysis tab, choose the channel, review the 8
   panels and the summary (RMS, MNF/MDF, fatigue). Bound the segment of interest.
   Generate the **PDF report**.
3. **MVC normalisation.** (Optional) Record a maximum contraction as reference, or
   use auto‑normalisation. In the MVC tab, normalise the task to % MVC and review
   the **muscular load (APDF)**. Generate its report.

### 7.2 Live load monitoring (ergonomics)

1. Connect and **Start recording**.
2. Press **Calibrate MVC** and perform a few seconds of maximum contraction.
3. Perform the task watching the **load bars**: if they enter orange (tiredness) or
   red (fatigue), intervene (pause, posture change).

### 7.3 Classroom session with several groups (followers on phones)

1. At each station, **one person** connects the BITalino and **Starts recording** on
   their PC.
2. They tick **“Broadcast to phones (classroom mode)”** and share the **address**
   with their group (everyone on the same Wi‑Fi).
3. The rest of the group opens that address on their phone and **follows** the signal,
   the calibration and the markers. When finished, each student can **download the
   session (CSV)** and, after the operator's analysis, the **report/results** on their
   phone.

## 9. Troubleshooting and FAQ

### Connection and device

**The device won't connect, or the LED stays yellow.**
- *BITalino*: check the MAC address and that the device is paired over Bluetooth.
  BITalino needs **Python ≤ 3.11** (it does not work on 3.12).
- *Arduino + MyoWare*: check the **COM port** (use *Refresh* to list the available
  ports) and the USB cable.
- The **watchdog** forces a disconnection if no data arrives within ~3 s; reconnect
  and try again.

### Signal quality

**Flat line / no signal.** Check electrode contact and the reference electrode, the
gain, and that the channel being shown is the right one.

**Strong 50 Hz mains interference / very noisy signal.** Ensure good electrode
contact (the notch filter removes 50 Hz, but poor contact amplifies noise); move
away from chargers and mains cables.

**Baseline drift or spikes.** Usually electrode/cable movement — secure the cables
and re‑clean the skin.

**"Possible saturation" warning.** The signal is hitting the ADC limits; lower the
gain or check the electrodes.

**"Flat baseline" warning.** Little or no signal — check electrode contact.

### Live load monitor and calibration

**"Calibration failed (no signal)."** You must be **recording** and contract
**maximally** during the calibration window.

**The bars say "not calibrated".** Press **Calibrate MVC** while recording.

**The live load looks wrong.** Recalibrate — the MVC reference is per recording
session and is reset when you start a new recording.

### Analysis and reports

**The EDF won't open, or the wrong channel is analysed.** Check the file and the
**channel name** (the channel list is filled from the EDF header).

**"Generate PDF report" does nothing.** Run an analysis first (the button enables
after a successful analysis).

**The report is unreadable for a long recording.** Choose a **shorter time range**
in the report dialog (it defaults to the on‑screen window).

### Classroom mode (followers on phones)

**The phone won't open the page.** Check it is on the **same Wi‑Fi network** as the
PC and that you type the address **exactly**, starting with **`http://`** (not
`https://`). Some browsers try to force HTTPS: type the `http://` explicitly.

**The address won't load on any phone.** The Windows *firewall* may block the port
the first time: allow **emgteach** on “private networks”. Also check the shown IP is
the classroom network's (not a VPN).

**Followers don't see the signal.** They must connect **after** classroom mode is on
and with a **recording in progress**; the view updates as data arrives.

**Results/downloads don't appear on the phone.** Results are pushed when the operator
**runs the analysis**; the PDF/CSV when they **generate/export** them on the Analysis
tab (with classroom mode on).

### Software and installation

**The app won't start.** Use **Python 3.10–3.12** in the project's virtual
environment. Python 3.13+ is not yet supported (the scientific stack has no wheels
for it).

## 10. Glossary

- **sEMG**: surface electromyography.
- **MUAP**: motor‑unit action potential.
- **Envelope**: smoothed curve of the activation amplitude.
- **RMS**: root mean square (amplitude).
- **iEMG**: integrated EMG (area under the envelope).
- **PSD**: power spectral density.
- **MNF / MDF**: mean / median frequency of the spectrum.
- **MVC**: maximum voluntary contraction.
- **% MVC**: amplitude expressed as a percentage of the MVC.
- **APDF**: amplitude probability distribution function (Jonsson's method).
- **Static / Median / Peak (P10/P50/P90)**: muscular load levels.
- **Onset**: contraction onset.
- **Classroom mode**: live broadcast of the session over the local network so
  students can follow it (read‑only) from their phone's browser.
- **EDF+**: standard biopotential file format.
