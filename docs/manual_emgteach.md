## 0. Executive summary (one page)

**emgteach** is a desktop application for the **acquisition, analysis and
normalisation of surface electromyography (EMG) signals** in the physiology
teaching laboratory. It lets you:

- **Record** muscle electrical activity in real time with a BITalino (or, in the
  single-muscle practical, an Arduino + MyoWare), on **one or two channels** and,
  when the practical asks for it, with the **accelerometer**.
- **Calibrate the maximum voluntary contraction inside the session itself**, with
  a wizard that writes it into the same file, and **monitor muscle load in real
  time** with tiredness and fatigue warnings.
- **Analyse** a recording the moment it is opened, with nothing to press: a
  **table with one row per contraction** (RMS, peak in % MVC, median frequency,
  electromechanical delay), **summary cards** with their reference ranges, the
  spectrum before and after the filter, and the **fatigue verdict** with the
  conditions it requires.
- **Compare two muscles** in % MVC and measure their **co-activation**
  (Falconer-Winter index) per manoeuvre.
- **Normalise** the task to the **MVC** and assess **muscle load** with Jonsson's
  method (ergonomics).
- **Export reproducible PDF reports** carrying the calibration, the tables and
  the cards: the deliverable of the practical.

Files are saved in the standard **EDF+** format (readable by MNE‑Python,
EDFbrowser and similar tools).

The application has **three tabs**: **Acquisition**, **Analysis** and **MVC
normalisation**. Before them sits the **practical selector**, with three options
and a coloured band naming the level (basic, intermediate, advanced); from it
follow the channel count, the accelerometer, what the recording asks for and
which measurements each tab offers (§4.1). A **five-step guide** and a **«?»** in
the corner of every box put the explanation where the question arises (§4.2).

## 1. Physiological basis of surface electromyography (sEMG)

### 1.1 What is measured

**Surface electromyography** records, through electrodes on the skin, the
**electrical activity generated by muscle fibres** as they contract. It does not
measure force or movement directly, but the **electrical command** the nervous
system sends to the muscle.

### 1.2 Origin of the signal

- The functional unit is the **motor unit**: a motor neuron and all the muscle
  fibres it innervates.
- When the motor neuron fires, its fibres depolarise and generate a **motor-unit
  action potential (MUAP)**.
- The surface electrode picks up the **sum** of the MUAPs of the nearby motor
  units. The result looks **stochastic**, with amplitudes from **tens of µV to a
  few mV**.

### 1.3 What the signal carries (amplitude and frequency)

- **Amplitude**: the **activation level** — how many motor units are recruited
  and how fast they fire. Related, non-linearly, to **force**. Quantified with the
  **envelope** or the **RMS**.
- **Frequency**: how the energy is spread across the spectrum; reflects the
  fibres' **conduction velocity** among other things, and is the basis of
  **fatigue detection** (§5.4). Studied with the **power spectral density (PSD)**
  and the **mean (MNF) and median (MDF) frequencies**.

### 1.4 Artefacts and noise (why we filter)

Mains interference at 50 Hz and its harmonics; electrode movement and baseline
drift (low frequency); high-frequency noise. Hence the filtering (§3).

## 2. Hardware and set-up

### 2.1 Supported devices

| Device | Connection | Practicals | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BITalino (revolution)** | Bluetooth (Classic/SPP, as a virtual COM port) | all three | Pair it in the operating system first; identify it by its MAC address. |
| **Arduino RedBoard Plus + MyoWare 2.0** | USB serial | single-muscle only | One channel and no accelerometer; firmware in the repository. |

Neither needs an optional extra or a compiler: both speak their device over
`pyserial`, on every supported Python version (3.10–3.12). The device selector
appears only in the single-muscle practical; the other two fix the BITalino and
say so.

### 2.2 Electrode placement

Two active electrodes over the **muscle belly**, aligned with the fibres and
**20 mm apart**; a **reference** on bone. Clean, dry skin. The forearm pair used in
the practicals (flexor and extensor carpi radialis), measured from the bony
landmarks, is described in
[`colocacion_electrodos_antebrazo_es.md`](colocacion_electrodos_antebrazo_es.md).

### 2.3 One or two channels (agonist / antagonist)

Two channels are for **agonist/antagonist pairs**: co-activation and coordination.
Each channel has a **label** (the muscle's name, in the order of the board's
inputs: Muscle 1 is recorded on A1) and a fixed **colour** (channel 1 blue,
channel 2 red) throughout. The channel count is fixed by the practical (§4.1).

### 2.4 Units and sampling

- Signals in **millivolts (mV)**, sampled at **1000 Hz**.
- The ADC reads an amplifier's output, so the conversion divides by its gain:

| Platform | Conversion | Full scale |
|---|---|---|
| BITalino | `EMG(mV) = (ADC / 2¹⁰ − 0.5) · VCC · 1000 / G`, VCC = 3.3 V, G = 1009 | **±1.635 mV** |
| Arduino + MyoWare 2.0 (RAW mode) | the same, with V_ref = 5 V and G = 200 | ±12.5 mV |

> Recordings made before the gain correction carry ±1.65 mV in their EDF header
> and are read back with it; the factor to bring them to the new scale is
> 1000/1009 = 0.99108. Nothing that is a ratio (% MVC, Jonsson levels, fatigue
> slopes) changes.

## 3. Signal-processing chain and its meaning

1. **50 Hz notch** — removes mains interference.
2. **20–450 Hz band-pass** — keeps the informative EMG band; discards baseline
   drift (< 20 Hz, electrode movement) and high-frequency noise.
3. **Rectification** — absolute value of the filtered signal.
4. **Envelope (5 Hz low-pass)** — a curve that follows the **activation level**.
5. **RMS** — amplitude in windows; the classic EMG amplitude indicator.

Only the **raw signal** is stored in the EDF; everything else is recomputed on
analysis, so the file stays small and always reproducible.

| Parameter | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Sampling rate | 1000 Hz | Samples per second |
| Band-pass | 20–450 Hz | Informative EMG band |
| Notch | 50 Hz | Mains interference (Europe) |
| Envelope cut-off | 5 Hz | Smoothing of the activation level |
| RMS window | 50 ms | RMS amplitude computation |
| Fatigue segment | 1 s (50 % overlap) | Windows for RMS/MDF over time |
| MVC reference window | 0.2 s | The best 0.2 s across the six calibration repetitions (§5.1) |
| Calibration | 3 × 4 s held + 3 × 1.5 s brief, after 10 s of warm-up | What the wizard asks per muscle |

## 4. The interface: first the practical, then the three tabs

### 4.1 Choosing the practical

In the top-right corner, on the same row as the language selector, a drop-down
with three options. It is the first control touched in a session: it fixes what
is recorded, and the three tabs configure themselves from it.

| Practical | Level (band) | EMG channels | Accelerometer | Device | Calibration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Single-muscle contraction** | basic (green) | 1 | no | BITalino or Arduino + MyoWare | with the «Calibrate MVC» button |
| **Agonist / antagonist contraction** | intermediate (amber) | 2 | no | BITalino | automatic on starting the recording |
| **Muscle kinematics** | advanced (purple) | 1 | yes | BITalino | with the «Calibrate MVC» button |

The practical **decides what is recorded**; it does not merely filter the view.
There is no channel-count selector and no accelerometer tick box on screen, and
the device selector appears only in the single-muscle practical: the other two
need a second channel or the accelerometer, which only the BITalino has, so the
box reads «Device: BITalino» and leaves the address editable.

**The level is the practical.** There is no "advanced options" tick: the fine
controls (envelope cut-off, region of interest, refined EDF, force-velocity study)
belong to the kinematics practical, which is advanced by its nature, and there
they take a row of their own. The other two do not show them.

Two behaviours worth knowing in advance:

1. **What a practical does not offer, it also unticks**, and restores the
   selection on returning. A panel left ticked would keep being drawn with no
   visible way to turn it off.
2. **Each practical opens on its own panels and «More panels…» reveals the
   rest**, minus those the recording cannot feed (a second muscle, the
   accelerometer).

**The recording overrides the practical.** Opening a one-channel EDF in the pair
practical warns how many channels the file has and proposes the practical that
fits it. A two-channel file turns the envelope comparison on by itself.

### 4.2 The interactive guide

A walkthrough pinned to the screen: it dims the rest, rings the control it talks
about and shows what it is and what it means physiologically, changing tabs on
its own when a step asks.

**Five steps** (seven in kinematics): choose the practical; connect, with the
device that practical admits and the order of the channels (Muscle 1 is recorded
on A1); record, with the maximum inside the recording; read the analysis; and why
everything is in % MVC. Kinematics adds the accelerometer placement and the
force-velocity study. What used to be fourteen to seventeen steps was split
between these five and the **«?»** of every box: a control's explanation appears
where the question arises, over that box, with the same panel the guide uses.

Offered at start-up with a **«Show this guide next time»** tick box, on by default:
a laboratory machine sees a different student most sessions, so the decision to
turn it off belongs to whoever owns the machine. That dialog names the sensor of
the chosen practical. The **«Guide»** button replays it; it refuses to start while
a recording is running.

**The guided steps of the analysis.** On opening a recording, the Analysis tab
points, with the same floating panel, at the next step: first «Calibration
repetitions…», then «Select fragments…». The box waits for the tab to be on screen
and leaves when the tab changes.

### 4.3 **Acquisition** tab (real time)

**Device configuration**
- Device type (BITalino or Arduino + MyoWare), in the single-muscle practical
  only; elsewhere the row reads «Device: BITalino».
- **MAC** address (BITalino) or **COM port** (Arduino), always editable; «Default»
  restores the saved one.
- **Output path and file**: the folder the EDF is written to.
- **Labels**: each muscle's name, in the order of the board's channels. In the
  pair the boxes start empty with a hint («Agonist, e.g. FCR»); left empty, the
  recording says «Agonist» and «Antagonist». In the other two practicals the
  channel is called «Muscle» and there is no box.
- **Test identifier**: a student, a pair, a bench or an attempt; goes into the EDF
  header and the report. It shares its row with the phone broadcast.

**Acquisition control**
- **Connect/Disconnect** and **Start/Stop recording**.
- A status indicator: red (disconnected), yellow (connected, no traffic), green
  (receiving). A *watchdog* forces a disconnection if the device stops sending.

**Live plots**: the **raw EMG signal** (mV, drawn to the device's own full scale)
and the **envelope**; a third one for the **accelerometer** in kinematics. With two
channels the raw plot is **stacked**, one lane per muscle, and the envelopes
**overlaid**. Scales with ▲▼, time window with a zoom drop-down and ◀▶.

**Event markers**
- **Auto-onset**, visible in all three practicals: a contraction's onset is marked
  when the envelope crosses a **threshold** equal to rest plus *k* times its noise;
  *k* sits beside it with its explanation («threshold = rest + k × noise; 3 is
  usual»). A smaller *k* catches lighter efforts and admits more false alarms
  (§5.6).
- There is no manual marking: asking the operator to type while watching the
  subject and the signal was a source of errors. Manoeuvres are recognised
  afterwards in the fragment editor, and the application fills in which muscle
  led each one.
- Markers are drawn live and stored as **EDF+ annotations**, like the phases of
  the session (warm-up, each calibration repetition, preparation, recording).

**Muscle load (live MVC)**
- **The calibration is a wizard**, always the same: 10 s of warm-up with two or
  three easy contractions; per muscle, **three sustained maximal contractions** of
  4 s («Contract FCR at maximum!») and **three brief maximal squeezes** of 1.5 s
  («Make a single, brief muscle contraction (a twitch) with the greatest force
  you can»), each with its countdown and 2 s of rest. In the pair practical the
  record button itself launches it before the task and then writes 5 s of
  preparation and the start of the recording; in the other two it is launched
  with **«Calibrate MVC»** while recording. Everything stays inside the same
  file, each repetition with its span marked (§5.1).
- On finishing each muscle, the wizard checks that the reference exceeds at least
  five times its resting level (otherwise it warns: not a maximal contraction)
  and, with two muscles, measures **how much the other channel read** during the
  maximum. Above 50 % of its own reference the panel ends on «Channels not
  separated».
- After calibrating, each channel shows a **load bar** with the **current % MVC**
  and its **colour zones**: green up to 40 %, amber (tiredness) up to 70 %, red
  (fatigue) above; and the live **P10/P50/P90** levels (§5.5).

**The accelerometer** (kinematics only). Recorded as one more channel, in `g`,
uncalibrated. Its box holds the analogue input selector (A1–A6, default A4), the
**«Find the ACC channel…»** button that locates it by reading the six inputs live,
and the placement selector: on the muscle (MMG) or on the moving segment (tremor,
force-velocity, electromechanical delay). Here too are **«Guided F-V…»**, which
directs the acquisition of a series of known loads, and **«Rehearse…»**, which runs
that procedure with no hardware.

### 4.5 **MVC normalisation** tab

**Entry panel.** The first time the tab is opened in each run of the application,

is made against something that does not give and why the reference is inside the

### 4.6 Following on phones

**One machine** (the computer driving the BITalino) broadcasts the live session
to the phones of the rest of the group, who **follow** in the browser with nothing
to install. Tick **«Broadcast to phones (in the laboratory)»** in Acquisition: a
local-network address `http://<PC-IP>:8070/?k=<code>`, a «Copy link» button and a
**QR**. Each activation generates a new session code; old links expire. Followers
see the envelope of each channel and the % MVC bar live, the calibration cues, and
can download the session (CSV) and, after the operator's analysis, the report
(PDF) and the results (CSV). Phones only watch; they cannot control the equipment.

## 5. Physiological concepts and metrics (interpretation guide)

### 5.1 Maximum voluntary contraction (MVC) and normalisation

The **MVC** is the maximal activation a subject can produce voluntarily in a
muscle. It is the **reference** for expressing any other activation as **% MVC**,
which is what makes muscles, subjects and sessions comparable: absolute
millivolts depend on skin impedance, electrode position and anatomy.

> **How the reference is measured.** It is the **best sustained 0.2 s** across the
> six calibration repetitions (three held maxima and three brief squeezes), with
> the window's resting level subtracted. Not the instantaneous peak, which one
> noise sample could set, nor the held half second, which sat on the plateau: a
> held contraction shows a peak at its start and then a plateau, and the task's
> brief efforts reach that peak. Measured on the plateau, the task beat the
> reference with the calibration correctly made (135 % at the bench). The task
> maximum and every contraction's peak are measured with the same 0.2 s window,
> so the comparison uses one yardstick.
>
> **How a maximum is made that is one.** Against something that does not give
> (the underside of the table edge), forearm rested, wrist about 20° towards the
> side opposite to the muscle's action; for the flexor, **with the fist closed**.
> Without resistance the muscle shortens at its fastest and, by the
> force-velocity relationship, develops its least force: a maximum in mid-air is
> submaximal by construction. With the hand open against the table the task
> reached 178 % of the flexor's reference; with the fist closed, 109 %.
>
> **How it is checked.** The «Task maximum» card in the analysis. Between 90 and
> 125 % is what correct calibrations give; from 150 % the application writes it
> in red, «not a maximum», on the card, over panel 9 and in the report, because
> every later percentage is wrong in the same proportion. Weak repetitions are
> discarded in «Calibration repetitions…» and the reference is recomputed.

### 5.2 Amplitude measures: RMS, iEMG, envelope

- **Envelope**: smoothed curve following the instantaneous activation.
- **RMS**: the standard amplitude measure in a window; relates to recruitment and,
  non-linearly, to force.
- **iEMG**: the area under the envelope; total activity over a period.

### 5.3 Spectral content: PSD, MNF and MDF

- **PSD**: how the energy is spread across frequencies.
- **MNF** and **MDF**: two summaries of the spectrum. The MDF splits the power in
  two equal halves; the MNF is the centre of gravity, always a little above the
  MDF because of the spectrum's tail. Both **fall with fatigue**.

### 5.4 Muscle fatigue and its detection

During a sustained contraction the fibres' conduction velocity falls and the
spectrum shifts towards lower frequencies: **MNF and MDF decrease over time**.

- The program computes the MDF in one-second windows (50 % overlap), **only in
  the windows in which the muscle was working**, and fits a line to MDF versus
  time (panel 7). The verdict follows that line:
  - **Fatigue detected**: negative slope and a line that explains something
    (R² ≥ 0.30) over at least four windows.
  - **Not detected**: the MDF holds or rises, with a line that fits.
  - **Not conclusive**: the line does not fit (R² < 0.30), or fewer than four
    windows. The norm in a series of brief contractions: the recording does not
    answer the question, which is not the same as answering "no".
- Fatigue only means something on a **sustained** contraction of some tens of
  seconds. Mixing rest and effort manufactures a slope from nothing (the MDF of
  rest is the amplifier's, very high), which is why the fit excludes quiet
  windows and why the fatigue exercise selects only the held stretch.
- Fatigue is often accompanied by a **rise in RMS** to keep the force (panel 8),
  but part of that rise comes from changing amplitude cancellation between
  potentials, not from recruitment; the specific proof is the MDF.

### 5.5 Ergonomic muscle load: Jonsson's method (APDF)

| Level | Percentile | Definition | Meaning | Jonsson's limit | The application uses |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Static** | P10 | Load exceeded **90 %** of the time | Near-continuous background load | 2–5 % MVC | 5 % |
| **Median** | P50 | Load exceeded **50 %** of the time | Typical working load | 10–14 % MVC | 14 % |
| **Peak** | P90 | Load exceeded **10 %** of the time | Recurrent high efforts | 50–70 % MVC | 70 % |

- Jonsson gave the limits as ranges: the low value for full-shift work and the
  high one for shorter tasks. The application uses the **upper end**, right for a
  one-minute laboratory task; an exercise simulating a working day should be read
  against the low end. Mean activation is compared with 10 %.
- Sustained exceedance is associated with fatigue and musculoskeletal risk, and
  the risk usually comes not from the peaks but from a high, sustained static
  level.
- The **live monitor** classifies the instantaneous load: green up to 40 % MVC,
  amber (tiredness) up to 70 %, red (fatigue) above. It measures something else
  than the APDF — the instant, not the distribution — and the two readings
  complement each other.

> The limits are guideline values from the literature, not clinical thresholds;
> they can be changed in the signal profile.

### 5.6 Contraction-onset detection

An onset is marked when the envelope exceeds the **mean of rest plus k standard
deviations** of rest (taken from the first second of the recording), with a 50 ms
minimum dwell and a 0.5 s refractory period. **k = 3** by default, the value
recommended by Hodges and Bui (1996) after comparing twenty-seven variants. A
recording therefore has to start with **a few seconds of rest**.

### 5.7 Co-activation, channel separation and electromechanical delay

**Co-activation index (Falconer and Winter, 1985).** Of all the activity recorded
in the two muscles, the fraction that was **shared**: 0 % if one worked and the
other did not, 100 % if both did the same throughout. Computed on the envelopes in
% MVC of each muscle, rest subtracted, **per window** — a window being a run of
consecutive contractions the application attributed to the same muscle (or to
both) in the fragment editor. When one muscle does not reach 5 % MVC on average in
the window, the row says **«not reported»** with its reason: in a clean flexion
the extensor is silent, and reporting "low co-activation" would be inventing a
measurement. In a firm grip both work and the index is high.

**Channel separation.** During one muscle's maximum the other channel is never
silent: partly real co-activation, partly the first muscle's signal conducted
through the tissue. The wizard measures it at calibration and writes it to the
event log and the report. With well-placed forearm electrodes it lies between 10
and 25 % of the channel's own reference; above 50 % the panel ends on **«Channels
not separated»**, because both pairs are then reading the same muscle.

**Electromechanical delay (EMD).** In the kinematics practical, with the
accelerometer on the moving segment, the contraction table gives for each effort
the time between the onset of the electrical signal and the onset of the
movement, both measured where each signal reaches a fifth of its own peak: calcium
release, cross-bridge formation and tensioning of the series elastic elements.
30–100 ms in healthy adults, 35–80 ms in voluntary contractions (Cavanagh and
Komi, 1979).

### 5.8 Reference ranges

Orientative values for surface EMG in healthy adults, shown in grey under the
cards and in the tables' «?». A value outside the range is a question, not a
fault.

| Measure | Orientative range | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mean frequency (MNF) | 80–170 Hz | most sEMG power lies between 50 and 150 Hz; MNF sits a little above MDF (Phinyomark, 2012) |
| Median frequency (MDF) | 60–150 Hz; forearm 90–150 | emgteach bench: FCR and ECR 86–127 Hz with a good montage, 176 Hz with a misplaced electrode |
| RMS at rest | ≈ 0.005–0.02 mV | amplifier and skin noise, ≥ 8 µV peak to peak at best (McManus, 2020) |
| RMS in effort | 0.1–1 mV; maxima up to ~1.5 mV | surface electrodes over limb muscles |
| Task effort | 20–80 % MVC | typical submaximal efforts |
| Task maximum with a sound calibration | 90–125 % MVC | emgteach bench sessions; red warning from 150 % |
| Antagonist co-activation | 5–10 % MVC in light efforts; 25–35 % in maxima | triceps during maximal elbow flexion ≈ 26 % MVC; finger extensor during wrist flexion at 75 % ≈ 15 % |
| Co-activation index | reciprocal: «not reported»; firm grip: 60–95 % | Falconer and Winter (1985); Ervilha (2012) on voluntary co-activation |
| Channel separation | ≤ 20–25 % of its own reference | emgteach bench with well-placed electrodes |
| Static, median, peak load | ≤ 2–5, 10–14, 50–70 % MVC (the application: 5, 14, 70) | Jonsson (1978, 1982) |
| Electromechanical delay | 30–100 ms; voluntary 35–80 | Cavanagh and Komi (1979): biceps 41 ± 13 ms, triceps 26 ± 11 ms |
| Physiological tremor | peak at 8–12 Hz | accelerometry of maintained posture |
| Onset threshold | rest + 3 standard deviations | Hodges and Bui (1996) |

## 6. File formats and reproducibility

### 6.1 EDF+ and reliable writing

Recordings are **EDF+**, readable by MNE‑Python and EDFbrowser. emgteach uses a
**buffered-write** pattern that avoids a silent corruption failure of real-time
EDF writing (described in a methodological publication by the author). Each
recording carries its own physical range in the header.

### 6.2 Annotations

Automatic onsets and the **phases of the session** (warm-up, each calibration
repetition with its muscle and number, preparation, start of the recording) are
stored as **EDF+ annotations**, together with each channel's MVC reference. The
analysis recomputes the reference from the marked spans, knows which part of the
file is the task and can discard a repetition without touching the signal. Four
annotation signals are reserved, since one holds about five per second and a
guided session writes bursts of them.

### 6.3 PDF reports

Self-contained and reproducible: the chosen graphs, the metrics, the calibration
section, the contraction table and a footer with the program version and the
date, saved next to the source EDF.

## 7. Typical workflows

Each begins by **choosing the practical** in the top-right selector.

### 7.1 Single-muscle contraction (basic)

1. Connect, type the test identifier, *Start recording*. If the exercise needs %
   MVC (graded effort, fatigue, load), press **«Calibrate MVC»** at once: warm-up
   and six maximal efforts against the table, fist closed. Then the task; onsets
   are marked by themselves. *Stop recording*.
2. Analysis runs on its own. Follow the two guided boxes: review the
   **calibration repetitions** and, in the **fragments**, keep only the task
   (unticking the six calibration efforts if there were any). Read the
   contraction table, the cards against their ranges, panels 1A, 2 and 3; for
   fatigue, panel 7 from «More panels…». Check the «Task maximum» card. Generate
   the **PDF report**.
3. MVC normalisation receives the recording and computes on its own. With
   «Select fragments…» keep only the task, and read P10, P50 and P90 against their
   limits.

### 7.2 Agonist / antagonist contraction (intermediate)

1. Two channels; the device box says «BITalino». Label the two muscles in channel
   order (FCR on A1, ECR on A2). On *Start recording* the wizard calibrates both
   muscles (fist closed for the flexor, back of the hand for the extensor),
   measures the channel separation and, after 5 s of preparation, opens the
   recording. Manoeuvres: flexions, extensions and a grip.
2. Analysis runs on its own. Review both muscles' repetitions and accept the
   fragments: the «Muscle» column already says who led each contraction, in %
   MVC of each. Read panel **9** (in % MVC, with the 100 % band), **3** and **7**
   with both muscles, the contraction table and the **co-activation table**, one
   row per manoeuvre.
3. MVC normalisation normalises one channel at a time with the reference the
   file carries for that muscle.

### 7.3 Muscle kinematics (advanced)

1. The accelerometer plot, its box and the fine controls appear. Choose the
   placement (on the muscle for MMG; on the moving segment for tremor,
   force-velocity and electromechanical delay); **«Find the ACC channel…»** if the
   trace does not respond; **«Rehearse…»** runs the procedure with no hardware.
2. **«Guided F-V…»** asks for the plan (loads, repetitions per load, preparation),
   starts the recording if needed and directs the series: a 3 s isometric maximum
   without load, then each repetition of each load, marked in the EDF with its
   load. For the electromechanical delay, quick single flexions suffice; for
   tremor, a held posture.
3. Analysis: panels **10**, **11** and **12**, the contraction table with the
   **EMD** column, and **«Force-velocity study…»**, which returns the
   load-velocity, force-velocity, power and recruitment curves.

### 7.4 Live load monitoring (ergonomics)

Connect, *Start recording*, **Calibrate MVC** (follow the wizard), then do the task
watching the **load bars**: amber above 40 %, red above 70 %. If any contraction
passes 100 %, the calibration was not maximal: repeat it.

### 7.5 Laboratory session with phones

At each station one person connects and records; ticks **«Broadcast to phones (in
the laboratory)»** and shares the link or the QR; the rest of the group follows and
downloads the session, the report and the results on their phones.

## 9. Troubleshooting and FAQ

### The interface does not offer what is wanted

**No device selector.** Only the single-muscle practical shows it; the other two
need the BITalino and the box says so. The address remains editable.

**No channel-count selector or accelerometer tick.** The **practical** fixes them
(§4.1).

**No fine controls (region, envelope cut-off, refined EDF).** They belong to the
kinematics practical. There is no "advanced options" tick.

**A panel disappeared and it was ticked.** The active practical does not open it
by default; **«More panels…»** reveals it, and returning to the practical that
offers it restores it ticked.

**The guided box of the analysis does not appear.** It waits for the Analysis
tab to be on screen; if it was closed, the «Next: …» line under the parameters
box says the same.

**The guide will not start.** Not while a recording is running.

### What the results say

**«Task maximum: … not a maximum», in red.** The recording exceeds 150 % of the
reference: the calibration was not a maximal contraction and every percentage is
high in the same proportion. Repeat it against the table, fist closed, holding
the four seconds; and check in «Calibration repetitions…» whether a weak
repetition is lowering the reference (§5.1).

**The co-activation table says «not reported».** One of the two muscles did not
reach 5 % MVC on average in that window: in a clean flexion or extension that is
the right answer. For a number, a manoeuvre in which both work is needed, such
as a firm grip (§5.7).

**The co-activation table says «whole recording».** No contraction has a name:
open «Select fragments…» and accept what it proposes.

**«Channels not separated» on finishing calibration.** During one muscle's
maximum the other channel read more than 50 % of its own reference. Move the
pairs apart towards the ulnar and dorsal edges, check each is over its belly,
rest the forearm (§5.7).

**«Fatigue: not conclusive».** The MDF line does not fit, or there are fewer than
four working windows: the recording does not answer the question. Expected in a
series of brief contractions. Fatigue needs a held contraction of tens of
seconds, and only that stretch selected (§5.4).

**A very high MDF (above 150 Hz) in one muscle.** Usually the electrode: too close
to the tendon or over another muscle. Compare with the other channel and move the
pair one or two centimetres towards the belly.

### Connection and signal

**The device won't connect.** BITalino: check the MAC and the Bluetooth pairing;
prefer the MAC to a COM number. Arduino: check the COM port and the cable. The
watchdog disconnects after ~3 s without data; reconnect.

**Flat line / "flat baseline" warning.** Electrode contact, reference, correct
channel. **Saturation warning.** Poor contact or an electrode over the tendon.
**50 Hz noise.** Contact; move away from chargers and mains cables.

### Phones

Same Wi-Fi; type `http://` explicitly; allow emgteach through the Windows firewall
on private networks; the link of a previous practical has expired by design;
followers must connect after the broadcast is on and while recording.

### Installation

Python 3.10–3.12; 3.13 is not supported yet.

## 10. Glossary

- **sEMG**: surface electromyography. **MUAP**: motor-unit action potential.
- **Envelope**, **RMS**, **iEMG**, **PSD**, **MNF / MDF**: as in §5.
- **MVC / % MVC**: maximum voluntary contraction and amplitude as its percentage.
- **Task maximum**: the strongest 0.2 s of the task as a percentage of the
  reference; above 150 %, the calibration was not maximal.
- **Calibration repetition**: each of the six maximal efforts per muscle (three
  held, three brief) marked in the file.
- **Co-activation index (Falconer-Winter)**: fraction of the two muscles'
  activity that was shared, per window; «not reported» when one did not work.
- **Channel separation**: what one channel reads of the other muscle during its
  maximum, in % of its own reference.
- **Electromechanical delay (EMD)**: time between the onset of the electrical
  signal and the onset of the movement.
- **APDF**, **static / median / peak (P10/P50/P90)**: Jonsson's load levels.
- **Practical**: the choice that configures the application, with its level.
